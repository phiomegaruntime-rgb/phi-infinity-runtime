# PHI-INFINITY — Personal Time, Flow, and Mechanical Progress

## 1. Status

This section is deliberately separated from the numerical implementation.

It records the temporal structure derived so far while preserving the open
problem that has not yet been solved.

No universal formula for proper time is introduced.

---

## 2. Reality is uninterrupted flow

PHI-INFINITY treats reality as uninterrupted transformation.

A fragment is not an ontologically static object followed by another static
object.

It is a distinguishable continuity inside flow.

Therefore:

\[
\text{sample equality}
\neq
\text{real immobility}.
\]

A finite runtime can report only whether transformation is distinguishable in
its current representation.

It cannot infer that reality itself has stopped.

---

## 3. Apparent immobility is relational

A fragment may appear almost motionless because its transformations are slow
relative to the temporal scale, spatial scale or resolution through which it
is accessed.

Another fragment may transform far faster than direct human perception.

Neither case requires the flow itself to stop.

The observable distinction depends on the relation between rhythms and access.

---

## 4. Persistence

Persistence is not identity between static states.

It is recognizable continuity through transformation:

\[
\text{PERSISTENCE}
=
\text{continuity compatible with transformation}.
\]

---

## 5. One mechanics, many rhythms

The mother mechanics remains one:

\[
\mathcal M.
\]

Different fragments do not require different fundamental mechanics.

They occupy different fields and configurations and may therefore have
different local temporal rhythms.

No privileged universal clock is required.

---

## 6. Neutral mechanical progress

The runtime uses:

\[
d\lambda_{\mathcal M}
\]

only as a numerical coordinate for traversing the mechanical trajectory.

Its code name is:

`mechanical_progress`

It is not:

- universal time;
- proper time;
- an absolute second;
- the age of reality;
- the total quantity of transformation.

The trajectory is primary.

Its numerical parametrization is secondary.

## 9. No global synchronization requirement

Fragments with different rhythms do not need to be forced into one universal
clock.

Their interactions must instead be compatible with the actual configurations,
fields and rhythms involved.

The principle is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{REALITY IS NOT REQUIRED TO BE GLOBALLY SYNCHRONIZED;}
\quad
\text{ITS INTERACTIONS MUST BE COHERENT.}
}
\]

Only interactions supported by the real configuration occur.

---

## 10. Infinite range of relative rhythms

Reality may contain fragments whose transformations are extremely slow relative
to human access and fragments whose transformations are extremely fast.

"Slow" and "fast" are relational descriptions.

The absence of directly visible change does not establish real immobility.

Likewise, an extremely rapid process does not stop when it is recorded.

---

A measurement has the structure:

\[
\text{source flow}
\rightarrow
\text{interaction}
\rightarrow
\text{trace}
\rightarrow
\text{later access}.
\]

The source continues transforming.

The record is a present consequence of an earlier interaction.

Therefore:

\[
\text{REALITY}
\neq
\text{ACCESS}
\neq
\text{RECORD}
\neq
\text{CURRENT SOURCE CONFIGURATION}.
\]

The runtime previously used:

\[
\|\Delta S\|
\]

as if it were proper time.

Tests showed that it may be zero while represented geometry changes, and that
its magnitude depends on the structural numerical update.

It is therefore retained only as:

\[
d\ell_S
=
\|\Delta S\|,
\]

a represented structural path increment.

The runtime keeps separate:

\[
d\ell_S,
\qquad
d\ell_x,
\qquad
d\ell_R.
\]

They are not collapsed into an invented scalar amount of total transformation.

---

`step()` does not represent an ontologically static instant.

It is a finite numerical traversal operation.

Convergence under subdivision supports interpretation of successive steps as
samples approaching a trajectory.

support it.

Gravity is NOT adopted as the universal PHI definition of time.

No existing gravitational formula is inserted as the PHI temporal law.
